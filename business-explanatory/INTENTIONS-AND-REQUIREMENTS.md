# Intentions & Requirements: Audience-Specific Dissemination Documents
### Living document — governs all drafts and revisions, evolves as we learn

---

## Purpose hierarchy (most to least important)

1. **Primary: research infrastructure.** These documents are kernel iterations shared with differentiated human audiences, inviting them to engage — as feedback-givers, advisory board members, clients, thought partners, collaborators, or "murderbot"-style stress-testers who try to break the ideas. The documents exist to generate real feedback from real differentiated perspectives and feed it back into the meta-models, the same iterative claims/kernel process already running with AI windows, now extended to humans.
2. **Secondary, much less important: marketing/shareable material for the company.** This function exists but should never distort the documents' actual content or framing. If a document reads like it's optimizing for conversion rather than honest inquiry, it has failed at its primary purpose.

## What these documents are NOT

- Not a financially-oriented enrollment pitch. No sales framing, no urgency, no conversion optimization.
- Not requiring NDAs or confidentiality markings — default is open/commons, not restricted. (Revisit only if a specific reason emerges; default stays open.)
- Not designed to convince people the work is valuable. Designed to let people who already have genuine alignment or curiosity self-identify, and to let people without genuine interest self-select out without friction or pressure.

## Tone requirements

- **Lead with something emotionally neutral and accessible — a story, an observation — that lets the reader arrive at their own feeling before the document names one for them.** Do not open with the sharpest emotional line (e.g. "show me the repair bill"). Earn it: let the reader recognize the pattern in something neutral first, then the sharper phrase mirrors a feeling they already have rather than introducing one, which reads as more genuine and less manipulative — and avoids creating bias even in readers who didn't already have it.
- Keep claims framed as open questions where the underlying material isn't yet validated. State explicitly what's checked against real prior art versus what's still hypothesis.
- Express genuine non-attachment: the project only works if each person self-selects what's actually right for them. QA is about getting the right people into the right roles, not maximizing headcount.
- At some point (not necessarily first draft), state plainly how epistemic integrity is being protected in the project's own evolution — including that Brenda has structured her position so that neither her nor the company's financial security depends on any single audience's response, specifically so ethical judgment isn't distorted by that pressure. This is a transparency offering, not a boast.
- Invite reciprocity: if this is useful to someone's own project, invite them to propose how, framed as a further test of the work's applicability and a way the collaboration can serve them too — not just serve the research.

## Structural template (per audience document)

Common shape across all versions, not uniform wording — see business-explanatory/README.md for the original version of this list:
- What we're doing, where we're coming from
- **"This is what Brenda's actually been doing for 20 years" — translated into this specific ecology's language.** Placed after the opening story, before the open questions/future-claims section. Most people know Brenda's work as "bodywork" and have no idea what that actually entails at a mechanistic level. This section pulls back the curtain: translates the real underlying practice (tracking causal cascades through a complex system, predicting how configurations behave under load, designing configurations that hold up against specific performance requirements, reverse-engineering stable architecture from unstable) into the ecology's own native language — systems-diagnostics language for systems thinkers, business-management metaphor for CEOs/VCs, etc. This is what makes the rest of the document's claims plausible: once the reader sees what she was actually doing, "she has something meaningful to say about systems science / engineering / business" stops being a stretch.
- How we're controlling and QAing that process
- How this validates what you (the specific audience) already believe/care about
- What this might add to what you already care about
- How this dovetails with other perspectives where there's been prior dissonance, with a pointer to the other audience-specific summaries
- Kept mostly as open questions, not settled claims

## Audience segmentation — corrected understanding

**Think in ecologies, not demographic audiences.** Each document speaks to
an ecology — a group of people who share real interdependence and mutual
stakes in each other's response, not just a shared label. For each
ecology: identify what everyone in it has in common first, then map the
demographic and psychographic variety *within* it, and draft inclusively
across that variety rather than writing to one subset and hoping the rest
follows. Example: CEOs and VCs are one ecology, not two — VCs need to know
CEOs will accept this and that it helps the founders they've invested in;
CEOs need to know operating this way will be appealing to their investors.
Speaking to only one side misses the actual interdependence that makes the
message land for either.

**"Systems thinkers" is a real, specific ecology, not a neutral generic
hub document for everyone.** Native systems thinkers — people who natively
think in patterns, connections, and regulatory ecologies, whether by
disposition or by hard-won professional experience, even without having
formal language for it — are their own distinct ecology, and the
systems-thinkers document should be written to and for them specifically,
not as a background-neutral base template other documents borrow from.

People who fit multiple ecologies get multiple documents, explicitly
framed: "these sections will repeat some content — reading each version
will show you how the perspectives converge." This overlap is a feature
for multi-ecology readers, not a flaw to eliminate.

## Feedback integration process (not yet designed — flagged as a real open task)

**Explicit requirement: the final decision on what feedback gets incorporated into the next draft must not rest on Brenda alone.** A real, non-biased process for integrating human feedback across iterations needs to be designed — this is unresolved and should be treated as its own work item, not assumed to be "Brenda reads feedback and decides," which would recreate the epistemic-monarchy problem already identified elsewhere in this project.
