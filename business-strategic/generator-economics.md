# Generator Economics — a candidate business model

**Status: 🟡 modal/candidate — coherent, not yet stress-tested**
**Source: P1, messages 1270–1271, ~archive p.1333–1336**

## The claim

Standard customer-retention logic treats a customer becoming self-sufficient
(no longer needing the product/service) as churn — a failure to retain.

The alternative model proposed here: optimize for producing customers who
become increasingly capable of generating value *without* continued
dependence on the company — "better nodes," not stickier customers. The
company's actual product isn't the direct solution to the customer's problem;
it's transferable generative capacity — something the customer can
independently reproduce, test, improve, and propagate afterward, the same way
a franchise or a good teacher's method outlives any single engagement.

Direct quote, worth preserving verbatim: *"The point isn't for us to generate
a sufficiently sophisticated explanation of Brenda. It's to extract enough
generative architecture from what Brenda already does that another node can
independently reproduce, test, improve, and propagate it."*

## Why it might be real business logic, not just nice philosophy

If revenue comes from (a) the initial coupling/diagnosis engagement, (b) the
speed/quality of transfer, and (c) the network effect of increasingly capable
former-customers referring or collaborating rather than one-off billing —
this isn't charity, it's a different unit economics model, closer to how
some high-end consulting or apprenticeship-based practices actually work than
to a subscription-retention SaaS model.

## What would need to be true for this to hold up as a real strategy

- The speed and cost of producing a "better node" (client capable of
  self-sufficient value generation) needs to fall as the underlying method
  matures — the transcript raises this as a real question ("how quickly does
  Brenda-requirement fall?") but doesn't yet answer it with data.
- Revenue needs a second act once dependency ends — referral, alumni network,
  case-study/credibility value, or licensing the method itself. Not yet
  specified in what's been extracted so far.
- Worth comparing directly against how apprenticeship-based professions
  (some legal, medical, and trades models) and open-source-adjacent
  consulting firms actually structure revenue around exactly this dynamic —
  real precedent to check against rather than inventing the model from
  scratch.

## Related epistemic point from the same passage (worth its own entry, noted here for now)

"There isn't deep-thing-causal / surface-thing-derivative — there's recursive
co-determination across levels," and the reframe of "find the real cause" as
"increase the system boundary until the relevant reciprocal causal
architecture becomes visible." This is close kin to **critical systems
heuristics** (C. West Churchman, later Werner Ulrich) — the established
systems-thinking tradition specifically about how "the cause" of a problem
depends on where you draw the system's boundary, and how boundary judgments
are themselves value-laden choices, not neutral facts. Worth reading directly
before treating this as original.
