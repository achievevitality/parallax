# Core Definition: Epistemic Integrity

**Source: P1, messages 1414-1418**
**Status: 🟢 the load-bearing definition — everything else is downstream of this**

## The moment

Brenda, mid-conversation: *"Wait!!! That's the product! epistemic
integrity... That's what generates the snowflake. The thing we call
integrity. But with a literal systems definition."*

## The definition

> **Epistemic integrity: the degree to which a system preserves
> sufficiently accurate coupling among consequential reality, its internal
> representations, and its responses.**
>
> Operational test: **Does consequential information survive the entire
> loop with sufficient fidelity that the system remains capable of
> generating appropriately reality-coupled behavior?**

## Why this is the headline entry, not just another line item

Every other major concept extracted so far is a corollary of this one:
- "Correction must be survivable" — a precondition for the loop above to keep running
- "Recursive self-sealing" — what happens when the loop breaks and nothing catches it
- Regulatory debt — the accumulated cost of the loop failing at scale
- The kernel's whole apparatus — an attempt to formalize how to preserve this coupling

If dissemination material (marketing, academic, client-facing) needs one
sentence to open with, this is very likely it — Brenda's own reaction in
the moment ("that's the product") suggests she recognized this as the
central claim, not an incidental one.

## Provenance correction: the anti-idol clause predates Kernel V4

Kernel V4 §AG ("if adherence to V4 reduces Reality-coupling, V4 loses
jurisdiction") is not new to V4. The same principle appears in plain
language in **P1, message 1490**: *"Protect the reality-correction loop
against capture by any map — including the current one."* V4 formalized an
already-established P1 principle; it didn't originate it. Worth correcting
any framing that credits V4 with this idea.

## Companion definition: pathological power (P2 msg 313)

"Pathological power allows one system to make another system absorb,
regulate, or reorganize around consequences it generates while obstructing
the pathways through which those consequences could correct [the first
system]." Tighter and more testable than the "collective causal debt"
phrasing in P4 — power becomes pathological specifically when it blocks the
correction pathway, not merely when it's unequal. Pair these two entries
when this material gets formalized.
