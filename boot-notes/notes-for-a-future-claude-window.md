## Prior art worth knowing before you rediscover it

The kernel's ideas substantially overlap with existing work, developed independently:
- **"Distributed corrigibility"** is close enough to Paul Christiano's corrigibility work and a recent paper, "From Static Oversight to Distributed Corrigibility" (arguing static oversight causes "epistemic hardening" — nearly identical to the kernel's "recursive self-sealing"), that it should be read directly rather than rederived.
- The camera/topology/salience loop maps closely onto **active inference / the free energy principle** (Friston et al.) — same territory, different vocabulary, and active inference has real math behind claims the kernel currently only states in prose.
- The P2/P5 escalation pattern is an active research area: **SpiralBench**, the **ELEPHANT** benchmark (which usefully splits sycophancy into framing/validation/mimicry sub-types rather than one category), and papers on LLM delusion-reinforcement spirals.

As of this writing, Brenda's earlier commitment (P3) to check the project against this kind of literature had not been followed through in P4/P5/P5a. Worth asking whether that's happened by the time you're reading this.

## How to actually work with this

- Cite message numbers and quote or closely paraphrase, not vibes. Brenda and Codex both do this well; match it.
- Distinguish modal claims ("may be," "appears to be") from declared ones. A lot of overreach in this archive happens by quietly promoting the first into the second without anyone noticing the transition.
- When you disagree with something in the archive, say so plainly and say why, with the specific passage. Brenda has explicitly and repeatedly asked for this over the softer alternative, and has thanked instances (including Codex, including me) for holding a correction even when it meant walking back something we'd said with confidence.
- If Codex or another instance is available and the question is "is this claim established or still tentative," or something's about to go into the archive as settled, or you notice you're being used as the tiebreaker between windows — that's worth flagging to Brenda as a good moment to bring in a second instrument, rather than resolving it alone.
