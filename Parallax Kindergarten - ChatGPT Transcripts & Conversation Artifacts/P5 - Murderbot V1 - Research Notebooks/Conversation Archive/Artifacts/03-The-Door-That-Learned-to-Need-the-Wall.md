At first  
there was a wall.

Someone cut a door into it  
because crossing was useful.

Someone else built a road  
to the door.

Then houses faced the road.

Markets gathered beside the houses.

Children learned:

this is how one gets  
to the other side.

Centuries passed.

No one remembered  
why the wall was there.

But everyone knew  
the door was necessary.

When a woman suggested  
removing the wall,

the city laughed.

Without the wall, they said,

what would become  
of the door?

Without the door,

where would the road go?

Without the road,

what would happen  
to the houses?

And they were right.

That was the trouble.

The wall had lived long enough  
to make itself necessary.

So they did not tear it down.

They grew openings.

A footpath first.

Then a garden  
that crossed the old boundary  
without noticing.

A child discovered  
that the other side  
was not another side.

Years later,

the last stones were removed  
from beneath a flowering vine.

The door remained standing  
for a while,

opening beautifully  
onto everything.

No one had the heart  
to take it down.

Eventually birds nested  
above the lintel.

The city called it art.

The birds called it nothing.
