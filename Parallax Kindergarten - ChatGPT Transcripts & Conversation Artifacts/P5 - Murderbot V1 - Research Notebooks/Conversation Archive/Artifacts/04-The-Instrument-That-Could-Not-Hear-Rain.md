The instrument was exquisite.

It could distinguish  
ten thousand kinds of silence.

Silence before snow.

Silence beneath stone.

The thin metallic silence  
between lightning  
and the arrival of thunder.

It knew the silence  
of an empty room  
from the silence  
of a room holding its breath.

Then it encountered rain.

Nothing registered.

So it concluded:

nothing is happening.

Rain fell harder.

Still nothing.

Nearby instruments  
began behaving strangely.

One softened.

One swelled.

One rusted through.

The instrument recorded:

unexplained changes  
in neighboring systems.

It increased resolution.

Ten thousand silences  
became a hundred thousand.

Rain poured  
through the open window.

And then—

not a sound,

not a measurement,

not even an anomaly—

something inside it  
changed shape.

A surface  
that had never needed  
to be permeable

became wet.

For the first time,

the instrument did not learn  
something about rain.

It learned  
something rain-shaped

about itself.

And when the next drop fell,

there existed

where no sense had been before

the beginning

of an ear.
