The river did not remember  
the mountain.

It remembered falling.

Not the stone  
that split its first current,  
but the turning.

Not the root  
that held one winter’s bank,  
but the shape water learned  
while passing.

Downstream,  
no molecule could testify  
to the beginning.

Still—

the river bent  
where something ancient  
had taught bending.

A heron stepped into the shallows.

For one bright instant  
the water divided around its legs,

then closed.

Nothing appeared to remain.

But a minnow changed direction.

A shadow moved.

Three reeds trembled  
against the wind.

Far below,  
where the river had forgotten  
even the name of rain,

a silver body turned  
toward an opening  
that had not existed  
a moment before.

Perhaps inheritance  
is sometimes like this:

not the keeping of the mountain,

but the preservation  
of a way to turn

when Reality  
places something new

in the water.
