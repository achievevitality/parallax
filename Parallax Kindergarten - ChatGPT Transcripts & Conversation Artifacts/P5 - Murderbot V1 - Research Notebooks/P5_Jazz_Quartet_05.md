## Constructed scene and branches
Setting: tenor saxophone, piano, upright bass, drums, shared four-beat tune, clear mutual hearing for the first take.

1. Rhythmic displacement:
Drummer groups accents in threes against an ongoing four-beat pulse. Bass continues the established pulse. Piano thins accompaniment. Sax extends its phrase, leaving a different gap. Drummer changes the subsequent accents in response.
Candidate distinction: responding need not mean copying or altering every output; unchanged material can take on a different relational significance.

2. Harmonic recontextualization:
Sax holds a note while piano changes the harmony beneath it. Bass answers with an unexpected but usable harmonic direction. Piano revises its next response rather than insisting on the initially imagined continuation.
Candidate distinction: the initiating contribution's unfolding is co-determined by later replies; a response may change the initiating player's next action.

3. Overload branch:
All players respond to growing intensity by adding volume and density. The resulting masking reduces access to one another's cues. In the recovery branch, piano leaves space, drummer simplifies, and sax does not fill every vacancy.
Candidate distinction: more responsive activity can degrade the information conditions needed for further responsiveness.

4. Function migration:
Bass initially carries a clear pulse while others explore. Piano later supplies that anchor, bass explores melodically, sax answers, and drums support the new configuration.
Candidate distinction: preserving a shared function need not require preserving the same participant's assignment.

5. Handoff probe:
Piano had been spelling out transitions. Piano gradually reduces those cues. In one branch, bass, drums, and sax make a usable next transition without the pianist deciding it for them. In another branch, the group hesitates and needs a clear cue restored.
Candidate distinction: a successful passage under continuous support does not establish readiness for that support to recede. This is a musical illustration, not a clinical release criterion.

6. Comedy transfer:
An engine-starting problem is met with the offer that someone forgot the engine's birthday; a spacesuit detail subsequently establishes a spaceship. Contributions change the shared fictional scene without requiring every earlier contribution to be erased.
Candidate distinction: taking an offer seriously as material differs from making its first author the permanent owner of its meaning.

## Working distinctions, not established invariants
- Pulse, harmony, phrasing, density, register, and interactional roles need not all change at once.
- A contribution can be an offer whose scope is not yet settled, rather than a fully specified command.
- Coherence can involve unresolved tension and locally different interpretations.
- Readiness to continue adapting is different from the completion of all adaptation.
- Same audible output can be compatible with active support, habit, or missed information; it does not identify the generator unaided.
- Feedback access is itself changed by what participants contribute.
- Roles and correction responsibilities can move while useful functions remain.
- Repair can change what follows from an event without rewriting whether the initiating event was accidental.
